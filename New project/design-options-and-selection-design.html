Design options and selection design

A hybrid visitor information system is the selected design.

The final design balances accessibility, reliability, cultural preservation and environmental sustainability.

Design review

Three design alternatives were reviewed before finalising the solution.

To improve visitor management in Lama Lama Country, the group reviewed several design alternatives before finalising the solution. Guided by the project requirements and stakeholder analysis, the core concern is not only providing basic information to visitors, but also ensuring the information is culturally respectful, easy to access, and sustainable in the long run.

Local rangers spend a large amount of time repeatedly explaining safety rules, camping advice, cultural protocols and restricted areas to visitors. This places heavy pressure on limited ranger manpower, particularly in remote locations where routine maintenance and on-site supervision are difficult to carry out. For this reason, the final design aims to lower the daily workload of rangers while preserving local cultural knowledge and guiding visitors to follow appropriate behaviour.

After reviewing the design requirements and limitations, three ideas were put forward: traditional physical signs, a fully app-based digital system, and a mix of both. After checking the advantages and drawbacks of each one, the combined hybrid design was selected as the most realistic and suitable choice.

Option 1

Pure physical signage system

This option would rely entirely on durable outdoor signs placed at campsites, entrances, walking tracks, and culturally sensitive areas. The signs would provide visitors with information about safety rules, permitted activities, cultural protocols, and directions.

Physical signs are simple and easy to understand, do not require internet access, mobile devices, or electricity, and can continue functioning during network failures and emergencies.

However, signs can only provide limited information, cannot easily provide layered cultural information or multilingual content, and may be damaged by heavy rain, cyclones, and flooding during the wet season. Physical signs are useful, but not enough as a complete solution.

Option 2

Fully digital application system

A fully digital app could provide maps, safety information, cultural guidance, visitor rules, images, audio, and videos. It would also make updates easier than replacing physical signs.

However, this option is less suitable for Lama Lama Country because internet access and infrastructure in the area are limited. Visitors may not download the app before arriving, and battery or signal issues could prevent access to important safety information.

Digital systems can support visitor education, but they still depend on technology and supporting infrastructure. For this reason, a fully app-based system was not selected.

Selected design

Hybrid visitor information system

The selected design combines physical signs with digital information that visitors can access through QR codes and downloadable offline files. Physical signage acts as the primary information source, letting visitors read safety guidelines and site regulations without internet or mobile devices.

QR codes offer optional additional content, including maps, cultural guidance and educational resources. The system follows an offline-first principle, so essential information remains accessible with poor signal or technical faults.

The layered information system also supports cultural protection by allowing the community to control what content is available to visitors. It reduces the repeated communication workload of rangers, remains cost-effective, and is better adapted to the remote environment of Lama Lama Country.
