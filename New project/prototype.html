Hybrid Visitor Information System

A practical offline-first system combining physical signage, QR access points and locally stored visitor information.

4. Detailed design

Physical signage works together with offline digital information.

The proposed solution is a hybrid visitor information system that combines physical signage with offline digital information. The system is designed specifically for the remote conditions of Lama Lama Country, where internet connectivity is often unreliable or unavailable. The purpose of the system is to improve visitor understanding, support respectful behaviour on Country, and reduce repeated explanations from Rangers. The design focuses on practicality, accessibility, cultural protection, and long-term sustainability.

Offline-first approach

Visitors access the core package before arrival.

Before arriving at Lama Lama Country, visitors are encouraged to download an offline information package through booking confirmation pages, email reminders, or locations with internet access such as Coen, Cairns, or Ranger Base. This package contains visitor rules, maps, safety guidance, recommended travel routes, and approved cultural information.

Once downloaded, the information can be accessed without requiring internet connectivity. This is important because many parts of Lama Lama Country have weak or no mobile network coverage.

Physical signage

Signs provide the first layer of guidance.

At entry points, visitors are introduced to the system through physical signage. These signs provide simplified maps, route information, safety warnings, and clear behavioural guidance. The signs use large icons, directional arrows, colour coding, and limited text to improve readability.

Numbered nodes and "You Are Here" maps help visitors navigate independently, while repeated core information means visitors can still complete a basic journey using physical signage alone.

Digital component

QR codes connect visitors to locally stored content.

Visitors who have downloaded the offline package can scan QR codes or enter node numbers to open additional information stored locally on their devices. The QR codes do not direct users to live webpages because internet access may not be available on-site. Instead, the QR codes connect visitors to pre-cached offline content using Progressive Web App technology and service workers.

Different visitor routes are colour-coded to improve navigation clarity. Green routes indicate basic visitor paths, blue routes indicate camping areas, and red routes identify culturally sensitive or restricted areas. Every major sign includes the same core information: current location, directional arrows, route colours, node numbers, and short safety reminders.

Community review

Cultural information remains community controlled.

Community concerns were an important consideration during the design process. One major concern was the protection of cultural knowledge and culturally sensitive locations. To address this issue, all cultural information shared through the system would be reviewed and approved by Yintjingga Aboriginal Corporation and relevant community stakeholders before publication.

Layered information access ensures that public visitor information is separated from culturally restricted knowledge.

Maintenance

The system is designed for remote maintenance.

Rangers can inspect signage during routine patrols, while simple repairs such as replacing QR stickers or updating printed signs can be completed locally without specialised technical staff. The use of durable materials also improves weather resistance and reduces replacement frequency.

Prototype

The prototype demonstrates how visitors use both physical and digital components.

A prototype of the Hybrid Visitor Information System was developed to demonstrate how visitors would interact with both the physical and digital components of the design in Lama Lama Country. The group focused on the parts that tourists would directly interact with, including the app UI, entrance signboards, QR codes, and offline information screens.

The app UI prototype was created using Figma and included offline maps, route selection, safety alerts, camping information, and cultural guidance. The physical signboard prototype displayed a "You are here" map, colour-coded routes, numbered stops, directional arrows, safety reminders, and QR codes.

Prototype testing focused on the app usage process. Testers simulated tourists and completed tasks without an internet connection: finding the map, understanding routes, viewing safety alerts, and finding information about culturally sensitive areas. Most users completed the basic operations smoothly, and the test also showed that pre-downloaded offline information was more reliable than live webpages in remote areas.

Feedback led to modifications including larger key buttons, stronger route colour indicators, earlier cultural reminders, and clearer safety information. Overall, the prototype confirmed that the hybrid visitor information system is practical, accessible, and suitable for the environmental and connectivity conditions of Lama Lama Country.
