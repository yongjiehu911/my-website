Project details

EWB 2026 Challenge and Lama Lama Country

This project is based on the EWB 2026 Challenge, focusing on Lama Lama Country in the Cape York region of Queensland, Australia. It aims to explore engineering solutions for sustainable development in remote environments (Engineers Without Borders Australia [EWB], 2026).

This area holds significant cultural, ecological, and social value, serving as an important site for Traditional Owners to conduct cultural practices, land management, and community life (EWB, 2026). However, due to its remote location, limited infrastructure, and the impact of seasonal climate conditions such as flooding during the wet season, the supply and management of services in the region face considerable challenges (EWB, 2019).

This makes the design of solutions adapted to the local environment particularly important, as engineering approaches must respond not only to technical constraints but also to cultural and environmental contexts (EWB, 2026).

Design Area: Eco-tourism and Visitor Experience

Project opportunity: Interpretation, Education and Cultural Grounding

HMW statement: How might we design a visitor information system in an environment with limited network access and resources that reduces the reliance on forest rangers, effectively guides visitor behavior, and protects cultural and ecological aspects?

Background

Tourism, environmental management and cultural preservation intersect.

Lama Lama Country, located in Cape York Peninsula, is a remote and culturally significant region where tourism, environmental management, and Indigenous cultural preservation intersect. The area attracts visitors for activities such as camping, fishing, and wildlife observation, but access to information is limited and often dependent on Rangers providing face-to-face guidance at the Port Stewart base (Lama Lama, n.d.).

This highlights a key challenge within the design area of interpretation, education, and cultural grounding: visitor understanding relies heavily on human interaction rather than scalable systems.

Stakeholders

Different groups need the system to do different jobs.

Rangers are responsible not only for visitor management but also for biodiversity conservation and cultural heritage protection, creating a high workload (Lama Lama, n.d.).

For Traditional Owners, maintaining control over cultural knowledge and ensuring respectful visitor behavior is essential to protecting Country. At the same time, visitors require clear, accessible information to navigate safely and act appropriately in a remote environment.

Research shows that Indigenous Ranger programs play a critical role in both environmental management and community wellbeing, reinforcing the importance of reducing unnecessary operational burden (Jones et al., 2018).

Current response and limitation

Current information channels are useful but hard to scale.

Currently, visitor information is delivered through basic signage, ranger briefings, and limited online resources. While these approaches provide accurate and culturally appropriate information, they are insufficient because they depend on ranger availability and consistent visitor engagement.

In remote contexts with limited infrastructure and connectivity, such reliance leads to gaps in communication and inconsistent visitor behavior.

Project aims and vision

A hybrid visitor information system.

This project aims to develop a hybrid visitor information system that combines physical signage with offline-accessible digital resources. The goal is to improve visitor education, reduce reliance on Rangers, and support the protection of cultural and environmental values.

By aligning with community-led approaches and local conditions, the project seeks to contribute to more sustainable and culturally respectful tourism on Lama Lama Country.
